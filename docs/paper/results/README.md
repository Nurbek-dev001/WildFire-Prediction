# Evidence pack for Results and Discussion

Source: the frozen public-data hindcast of the article's Methods (`runs/public_hindcast`),
regenerated by `scripts/paper_figures.py` (tables T1-T15, figures F1-F13) and
`scripts/paper_ablation.py` (ablation, T16, F14). Every value is a real model result;
nothing is simulated. These are notes of facts and numbers; the article text is yours to write.

Design in one line: 5 km equal-area grid, 110,129 cells; target = first VIIRS S-NPP detection
of a new event (2 km / 48 h linkage) in a cell without detections in the previous 48 h;
development 2015-2021 (rolling-origin validation 2019, 2020, 2021), sigmoid calibration and
thresholds on 2022, untouched test 2023-2024; ~10 sampled negatives per positive with
inverse-inclusion weights; 1000 space-time block bootstrap resamples (100 km x 30 days).

## Research question 1: does the combined model beat seasonal and weather-only references?

**Yes, by a wide and statistically clear margin; most of the gain comes from land surface,
human and seasonal information, not from weather.**

| Model (test 2023-2024) | AP | AP / prevalence | ROC-AUC | Top-5% capture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| XGBoost | 0.0204 | 104x | 0.924 | 55.6% |
| CatBoost | 0.0175 | 89x | 0.908 | 50.2% |
| Random Forest | 0.0070 | 36x | 0.879 | 49.2% |
| Logistic regression | 0.0010 | 5x | 0.793 | 25.6% |
| Seasonal frequency | 0.0006 | 3x | 0.731 | 14.3% |
| Weather-only logistic | 0.0004 | 2x | 0.662 | 8.4% |

- Prevalence 0.000196 (no-skill AP). XGBoost minus seasonal AP: +0.0161 to +0.0306 (95% CI);
  minus weather-only: +0.0162 to +0.0307; minus logistic +0.0156 to +0.0301; minus random
  forest +0.0095 to +0.0231; minus CatBoost -0.0017 to +0.0124 (not separable). T2, T5, F1, F2.
- Model selection agreed with the test: XGBoost best in every validation year (mean AP 0.0191),
  CatBoost second (0.0110). T4, F4.
- Ablation (T16, F14; XGBoost refitted with the frozen hyperparameters, so its own full
  model scores AP 0.0234 on the test years, not 0.0204). AP lost when a group is removed,
  paired 95% CI:
  - terrain 0.0154 (0.0118 to 0.0233), land cover 0.0094 (0.0021 to 0.0134), human pressure
    0.0079 (0.0002 to 0.0123), season 0.0045 (0.0006 to 0.0076): all significant; terrain is
    also the largest loss in rolling validation (mean AP 0.0096 against 0.0159);
  - vegetation 0.0023 (-0.0028 to 0.0062) and 7/14-day weather -0.0007 (-0.0082 to 0.0029):
    not distinguishable from zero;
  - daily weather of D-7: -0.0068 (-0.0144 to 0.0011), i.e. removing it if anything helps;
  - weather alone (XGBoost on the 10 weather inputs): AP 0.0006, a loss of 0.0228 (0.0184 to
    0.0321), as low as the weather-only logistic reference.
- Independent confirmation from the later operational study (2020-2025 rolling origins): the
  Canadian Fire Weather Index alone has mean AP 0.0003 (near chance); climatology 0.0107;
  yesterday's nearby fires 0.0088 (`runs/model_v7/experiments/baselines`).
- Why weather is weak here: in this design the daily weather is ERA5 of day D-7 (the archive
  is published about five days late, so nothing newer is available when a forecast is made),
  and ignition in Kazakhstan is dominated by people (stubble and pasture burning, settlements,
  roads); where and when people burn is captured by population, land cover and calendar.

## Research question 2: future years and geographically separate regions

**Temporal transfer is stable; geographic transfer works but with much lower skill and
poorer calibration.**

- Temporal: validation AP 0.019 (2019-2021) -> test AP 0.0204 (2023-2024); ROC-AUC 0.924 in
  both test years; AP 0.0192 in 2023 and 0.0277 in 2024 (T3). The ranking of models is the
  same in validation and test.
- Spatial (five folds of 100 km blocks with a 25 km buffer, development period, T6, F12):
  mean AP 0.0039 (15x prevalence, range 6-37x), ROC-AUC 0.87 (0.81-0.89), negative Brier
  skill in 4 of 5 folds. A held-out region keeps a useful ranking but loses most precision.
- Caveat: hyperparameters were chosen globally on 2015-2021, so the spatial audit is
  conditional, not a fully nested independent spatial test.
- Later years (same platform, newer models): independent 2025 test of the stacked model and
  2026 test of v6 (AP 0.044, recall 15.3% at precision 8.7%, `platform/dist/v6`) show the
  approach keeps working on years after 2024.

## Research question 3: calibration and explanations

**Calibrated probabilities are reliable on average but not for decisions at the high end;
the explanations are dominated by human, seasonal and land-surface predictors and change
across land-cover groups.**

- Brier skill against the seasonal reference is ~0 for every model (XGBoost +0.0002, CatBoost
  +0.008); ECE is tiny (8e-5) only because almost all cell-days have probabilities near zero
  (T2). Reliability (F3, T11): the four map classes fixed on 2022 order risk correctly
  (observed 0.000009 -> 0.000067 -> 0.00030 -> 0.0026) but over-predict by 1.3-1.8x; the few
  highest bins (0.1-0.3) under-predict (0.128 shown vs 0.283 observed).
- At the 2022 threshold (0.047) XGBoost alerts are right 9.8% of the time and catch 5.2% of
  starts (FPR 0.009%); 90% of alerts are false (false-discovery proportion). Accuracy 0.9997
  is uninformative at this prevalence.
- Global SHAP (F5): population density 0.59, month (cos) 0.41, 14-day temperature 0.40,
  cropland 0.33, elevation 0.30, month (sin) 0.29; daily weather variables are small.
- By land cover (F6, T12): cropland is driven by antecedent weather (1.15) and cover (0.81),
  consistent with weather-timed stubble burning; grassland by human pressure (0.65), season
  (0.61) and antecedent weather (0.57); forest by cover (0.94), antecedent weather (0.81)
  and human pressure (0.73). Contributions are predictive associations in uncalibrated
  log-odds, not causes.
- By region (T8, F9, T13): highest AP in Atyrau, Aktobe and East Kazakhstan; lowest in
  Akmola, Kostanay and North Kazakhstan (AP 0.0006-0.003), the cropland north where burning
  is widespread and less predictable cell by cell. Part of the high western scores likely
  reflects persistent gas flares not fully removed by the industrial mask (see limitations).

## Case: early June 2023 (the motivating Abay fire)

F13, T15. Across all of Kazakhstan, 19-31% of each day's new starts fell inside that day's
top 5% of cells. In East Kazakhstan (which then included the Abay Region) the start near
Semey on 8 June was at the 78th percentile, i.e. not flagged; four of the seven other
eastern starts on 8-9 June were at the 96th-100th percentile. The model would not have singled out the start of
the Semei Ormany fire; this supports the introduction's caution that a forecast cannot be
shown to have prevented the disaster.

## Limitations for the Discussion

1. Labels: one sensor (S-NPP), active-fire detections, not ignitions; cloud cover and small
   fires missed; agricultural burning cannot be separated (cropland reported separately).
2. Persistent industrial heat sources: the frozen mask removed known industrial cells, but
   later work found 44 more persistent sources that inflate some regional scores.
3. Weather lag of seven days; no forecast weather in this experiment (the operational v7
   adds GFS forecasts for the target day and roughly doubles recall at fixed precision).
4. Spatial audit conditional on globally tuned hyperparameters; 5 rather than 50 trials.
5. Probabilities need recalibration before use as absolute chances (BSS ~0).
6. Forest fires are rare at 5 km (5 test-period starts in forest-dominant cells).

## Comparison hooks (from the article's references)

- Ouazri et al. [21] report 92.5% accuracy and ROC-AUC 0.972 (Morocco): our accuracy is 0.9997
  and ROC-AUC 0.924, but at prevalence 0.0002 accuracy is meaningless and AP (0.020, 104x
  chance) is the informative measure; distributions differ.
- Choi and Chae [22] give per-fire PR-AUC 0.429 for burn probability given an ignition, a far
  less rare target than ours; their calibration point (overconfidence at high probability)
  matches our high-bin miscalibration.
- Iban and Aksu [16] and the human-access discussion (Section 2.1): our strongest SHAP
  predictor is population density, consistent with human-driven ignition.
- Babu et al. [7], Chepashev et al. [8]: static hazard maps for Kazakhstan; our results add
  daily, calibrated and temporally tested probabilities, and show static land surface and
  human layers carry most of the signal while lagged weather adds little.
- Camacho et al. [26]: rare-event design; our sampled negatives with inverse weights and
  unbalanced evaluation follow this concern.
